Why I Write Every Morning (And Why You Might Not Need The)

Last Tuesday I sat down at 6:47 AM, coffee gone cold, and stared at a blank document for what felt like an hour. Nothing came. I wrote two sentences, deleted them, wrote them again. This is the glamorous writing life nobody posts about.

I've kept a daily writing habit for four years now, and people keep asking whether it's "worth it." Here's what I actually think, no productivity gospel attached.

The first thing writing does is it catches lazy thinking. An idea that feels airtight in the shower usually falls apart the moment you have to put one sentence after another. If you can't write it, you probably haven't thought it — you've just felt it. That's the one claim I'd defend in any room.

The second thing: published writing compounds. A talk disappears; a post can get found by someone three years later. I still get emails about something I wrote in 2022. Nothing else I do has that shape.

Third, and this one is softer: showing up daily lowers the cost of showing up. I no longer wait for mood or inspiration, because the deal I made with myself doesn't include them. Whether that's freedom or just a treadmill, I genuinely can't tell anymore.

Honestly, most of my morning pages are mediocre. Some weeks I delete more than I keep. Maybe the habit works for me because I'm the kind of person who needs a small daily appointment with his own thoughts. You might be different. You might think better while walking. I'm not selling a program. I'm just describing what one person does at 6:47 in the morning.
