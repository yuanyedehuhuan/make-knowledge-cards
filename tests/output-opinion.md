# Knowledge Cards: Why I Write Every Morning (And Why You Might Not Need The)

Source: sample-opinion.txt · 5 cards

## Card 1: Writing Exposes Lazy Thinking

- **Core knowledge:** Writing forces an idea to stand as one sentence placed after another; if you cannot write an idea, you have probably only felt it, not actually thought it.
- **Explanation:** An idea that feels airtight in the shower usually falls apart the moment it has to be expressed in connected sentences. Putting thought on the page reveals gaps that feeling alone hides. The author calls this the one claim about writing he would defend in any room.
- **Example / Self-test:** An idea seems flawless while he is in the shower, but when he tries to write it out sentence by sentence, it collapses — the writing has caught lazy thinking.

## Card 2: Published Writing Compounds

- **Core knowledge:** Published writing persists and can be found years later, so its value accumulates in a way a live talk does not.
- **Explanation:** A talk disappears after it is given, whereas a published post can be discovered by someone three years later. Because written work keeps resurfacing over time, nothing else the author does has "that shape" of compounding return.
- **Example / Self-test:** He still receives emails about something he wrote back in 2022 — readers finding the piece years after he published it.

## Card 3: Showing Up Daily Lowers Its Cost

- **Core knowledge:** Making writing a daily appointment lowers the cost of starting, because the writer no longer waits for mood or inspiration before working.
- **Explanation:** The deal he made with himself does not include mood or inspiration, so he writes whether or not he feels ready. He is openly unsure how to judge the result: whether this freedom from waiting is genuine freedom or merely a treadmill, he genuinely cannot tell anymore.
- **Example / Self-test:** One Tuesday he sat down at 6:47 AM, wrote two sentences, deleted them, and wrote them again — the session happened even though nothing was coming.

## Card 4: Most Daily Pages Are Mediocre

- **Core knowledge:** A daily writing habit does not make most of the output good; much of it is mediocre and ends up deleted.
- **Explanation:** The author states honestly that most of his morning pages are mediocre, and some weeks he deletes more than he keeps. The value of the habit is therefore in showing up and practicing, not in expecting every session to produce keepers.
- **Example / Self-test:** In some weeks, the amount of morning writing he throws away exceeds the amount he keeps.

## Card 5: Habit Fit Is Personal

- **Core knowledge:** Whether a daily writing habit is worth it depends on the person; a method that fits one person's mind may not fit another's.
- **Explanation:** The author suspects the habit works for him because he is the kind of person who needs a small daily appointment with his own thoughts, and he recognizes that someone else may think better in a different way. He is describing his own practice, not prescribing a universal program.
- **Example / Self-test:** He suggests a reader might think better while walking, and stresses that he is not selling a program — just describing what one person does at 6:47 in the morning.
